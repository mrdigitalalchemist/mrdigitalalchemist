## 🔬 How I work

<details>
<summary><b>The verifier principle — no finding ships unproven</b></summary>

<br/>

Automated scanners are judged on recall, so they optimise for it — and hand you a
PDF of maybes. Every finding our offensive engine produces goes to a **second,
independent agent** that has to re-exploit it from scratch. If it can't, the
finding doesn't exist.

The result is a report where the severity column means something, and nobody on
the client side spends a week triaging noise.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Defense that survives contact with real traffic</b></summary>

<br/>

Our edge connector sits in the actual request path — so it can't be a demo. It
enforces IP and geo rules, rate limits, SQLi/XSS signatures and bot detection,
then answers suspicious traffic with a **proof-of-work challenge** instead of a
third-party captcha.

Verified search crawlers are identified by **reverse DNS**, not user-agent, so
they pass through untouched. Your SEO survives your security.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Autonomy with a leash — and a receipt</b></summary>

<br/>

An autonomous red team is only deployable if you can bound it and prove what it
did. Activated-X ships with hard **scope enforcement**, three explicit autonomy
modes, and a **tamper-evident audit log** of every action taken.

Freedom to act, on a leash you control, with a receipt for everything.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Teaching red team</b></summary>

<br/>

The gap in security education isn't tooling — it's method. Anyone can run a
scanner; far fewer can build a hypothesis, chain a finding into impact, and
write it up so a business can act on it.

That's what I teach, and it's the same discipline the platform automates.

</details>
